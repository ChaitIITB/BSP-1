CS6101 · Project report

Ranking researchers within a field

Pilot on a closed Information Retrieval (IR) citation graph. This report puts every comparison from the progress presentation in one place.

Summary

  • Data: 14,462 IR papers (five venues, 2000–2022), 21,362 authors, 54,351 citation edges from OpenAlex.
  • Metric (Approach 1): each paper's share of the field's citations in a short window after publication, summed per author. It ranks well-known IR researchers first (Croft, Zhai, Dumais).
  • Parameters: the ranking is robust to the window length Δ and to the cutoff year. Citations in this field arrive with a median of 3 years, which supports Δ = 3.
  • Sources: OpenAlex gives four times as many edges as Semantic Scholar for the same papers, so it is the better base here.
  • Fine-tuned LLM (Approach 4): learned the ranking task (98.6% valid answers, top-1 0.51 vs 0.17 random) but did not beat a simple "more papers = higher rank" rule (top-1 0.62).
  • Not done: real LLM runs of Approaches 2, 3 and 5, and any independent ground truth. Every comparison below is between our own rankings.

2. Metric and data

Approach 1. For a paper born in year y: share = citations in [y, y+Δ] ÷ all citations made inside the set during [y, y+Δ]. Papers born 2006–2014 count. A person's score is the sum of their paper shares, split equally between co-authors; people with 3+ papers are kept (1,430 authors). Years only, not months.

Dataset. CIKM 6,707, SIGIR 5,076, WSDM 1,384, TOIS 746, Information Retrieval 549 papers. Of 345,866 reference entries, 16% point inside the set. Rebuild script: build_ir.py (reproduces the stored files exactly).

Noise floor: dropping 20% of edges moves A1's ρ by at most 0.014 (20 runs). Normalizing by output removes the tie to paper count (0.61 → 0.33) but not age bias, and it costs 0.083 in agreement with future citations, about ten noise floors. M1 agrees with plain per-paper citations at ρ = 0.987, so once divided, the field-share denominator adds nothing. On Semantic Scholar the same pattern holds for M1 (ρ 0.502 → 0.456; age bias −0.002 → +0.010). Shrinkage (M4) is the worst variant — with a 3-paper minimum and k = 3 it pulls every short record toward the mean.

Verdict: report M1 beside A1 as "typical output quality", not as A1's replacement. Age bias lives in the birth window (longer exposure for earlier papers), not in output volume, so a denominator cannot fix it.

7b. Citation deflator

Each citation received in year t is multiplied by D(t) = R(2021)/R(t), with R(t) the references per pool paper of year t. R rises from 18.4 (2005) to 32.1 (2021), so a 2005 citation counts 1.74× and a 2021 citation 1.00×. Pre-2005 years are undefined and dropped; no in-window edge falls in one. With D(t) = 1 set everywhere the pipeline reproduces A1 exactly (ρ = 1.00000000).

D1 costs almost no accuracy (0.014 ρ, against M1's 0.083) and barely reorders: the first nine ranks are identical and only two adjacent swaps occur in the top 12. But age bias moves away from zero, as it does on Semantic Scholar (−0.002 → −0.008). Deflated per-paper counts (B1′) are far worse on age bias, so under deflation the field share is still doing real work. The prediction that the deflator would reshuffle old and young authors (top-100 overlap below 70) was wrong: D(t) is close to flat after 2012 and only trims mid-career tails. Verdict: age bias is not citation inflation of this form; the deflator is a fair reporting correction, not an age-bias fix.

8. What the comparisons say together

  • The A1 ranking is stable under changes to Δ and cutoff and under random edge loss, so its settings are not the weak point.
  • The source matters more: Semantic Scholar's thin graph ties 82% of authors.
  • The hand-written variants (A2, A3) disagree with each other, so the ranking depends on design choices we cannot yet adjudicate without ground truth.
  • The fine-tuned model reproduces part of A1 from text, but a count of papers does better. It is not yet evidence that language models add information.
  • Normalizing the score by paper count trades volume dependence for accuracy and does not remove age bias; it is a second view, not a better metric.
  • Neither per-paper normalisation nor the citation deflator reduces age bias; the deflator makes it slightly worse while costing almost no accuracy. Both point at the birth window as the cause.

Limits: no independent ground truth; years not months; OpenAlex edges unverified; single field; one fine-tuning run.

Appendix: the queries, rules and prompts behind every result

Parameters: Δ = 3, birth years 2006–2014 (cutoff 2014), minimum 3 papers. Tables: edges(cited, citing, y_cited, y_citing), authors(paper, year, author, n) (n = number of authors on the paper), cau(paper, author) (authors of each paper, for self-citation removal). Code: scripts/approaches.py.

A. Approach 1 (reference implementation, a1())

CREATE TABLE a1_share AS
WITH tot AS (                       -- all in-set citations made in each year
  SELECT y_citing AS yr, COUNT(*) AS n FROM edges GROUP BY 1),
den AS (                            -- field total for the window [y, y+3]
  SELECT y.year, SUM(t.n) AS d
  FROM (SELECT DISTINCT year FROM authors WHERE year BETWEEN 2006 AND 2014) y
  JOIN tot t ON t.yr BETWEEN y.year AND y.year + 3 GROUP BY 1)
SELECT e.cited AS paper, e.y_cited AS year,
       COUNT(*) * 1.0 / ANY_VALUE(d.d) AS share
FROM edges e JOIN den d ON d.year = e.y_cited
WHERE e.y_citing <= e.y_cited + 3 AND e.y_cited BETWEEN 2006 AND 2014
GROUP BY 1, 2;

-- author score: uncited papers add 0 but still count toward the 3-paper minimum
SELECT a.author, SUM(COALESCE(s.share, 0) / a.n) AS score,
       COUNT(*) AS n_papers, SUM(COALESCE(s.c, 0)) AS raw_cites
FROM authors a LEFT JOIN a1_share s ON s.paper = a.paper
WHERE a.year BETWEEN 2006 AND 2014
GROUP BY 1 HAVING COUNT(*) >= 3;

B. Approach 2 (hand-written SQL stand-in, sql/a2_v1.sql)

The comment block at the top is the plain-language prompt an LLM would receive. a2_llm.py sends the schema and that description and asks for one DuckDB query; with --mock it replays this file. No real LLM has been called yet.

-- Approach 2, single shot. Prompt given to the LLM: the schema below plus the plain-language metric:
--   "For each paper p in the field, take the citations p receives within DELTA years of its publication,
--    divided by all citations the field's papers receive in that same set of years. Ignore papers published after
--    CUTOFF. Give each author the sum of their papers' shares (split equally between co-authors) and rank authors."
-- Schema: edges(cited, citing, y_cited, y_citing)  authors(paper, year, author, n)
-- Parameters substituted by the runner: {DELTA}, {CUTOFF}, {MIN_PAPERS}
WITH field_cites AS (
  SELECT y_citing AS yr, COUNT(*) AS total FROM edges GROUP BY y_citing
),
window_total AS (
  SELECT p.year, SUM(f.total) AS total
  FROM (SELECT DISTINCT year FROM authors) p
  JOIN field_cites f ON f.yr BETWEEN p.year AND p.year + {DELTA}
  GROUP BY p.year
),
paper_share AS (
  SELECT e.cited AS paper, e.y_cited AS year,
         COUNT(*) * 1.0 / w.total AS share
  FROM edges e JOIN window_total w ON w.year = e.y_cited
  WHERE e.y_citing - e.y_cited <= {DELTA} AND e.y_cited <= {CUTOFF}
  GROUP BY e.cited, e.y_cited, w.total
)
SELECT a.author, SUM(s.share / a.n) AS score, COUNT(*) AS n_papers
FROM paper_share s JOIN authors a ON a.paper = s.paper
GROUP BY a.author
HAVING COUNT(*) >= {MIN_PAPERS}
ORDER BY score DESC;

C. Approach 3 (hand-coded refinement rounds, a3())

-- self-citation filter used in A3 and the evaluation
AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM authors x JOIN cau y
                ON y.paper = e.citing AND y.author = x.author
                WHERE x.paper = e.cited)

Round 1: count in-window citations per paper with the self-citation filter above; author score = total in-window citations over the author's papers (no co-author split, no field share).
Round 2: per paper, percentile rank among papers of the same birth year (ties = average rank);
         author score = (mean percentile x n_papers + 0.5 x 5) / (n_papers + 5)   -- shrinks small samples toward the middle
Round 3: same, but uncited papers get percentile 0 (ties = minimum rank); shrink toward the field mean percentile
Round 4: same percentile as round 3; author score = mean percentile x n_papers  (volume-aware, no shrinkage)
(A3 in the tables above = round 3.)
